Prompting

How to get the most out of Copilot

A great prompt can have up to four parts — Goal, Context, Source, and Expectations. Only the goal is required; add the others to sharpen the result. The more direction you give, the more useful the answer.

1 The four building blocks

Goal · required

What you want Copilot to do. Start with a verb — "Summarize…", "Draft…", "Create a table…"

Context

Why you're asking and who it's for — "…for my executive ahead of the Business Operations Review."

Source

What Copilot should use. Point it at emails, a meeting, or a file by typing / — "…based on the Atlas launch emails this week."

Expectations

The shape of the answer — format, length, tone — "…in five bullets, friendly tone, under 150 words."

2 Put it together

Start with just a goal, then layer in the other parts to go from a generic answer to exactly what you need.

Just a goal → "Summarize my emails."
Goal + Context + Source + Expectations
Summarize the emails from the Atlas launch team over the past week so I can brief my executive ahead of tomorrow's Business Operations Review. Group them into what needs a decision and what's just an update, and keep it to five short bullet points.
Why it works: it names the goal (summarize), the source (the Atlas launch emails this week), the context (a brief for the executive before the Business Operations Review), and the expectations (grouped, five bullets). Every prompt on the Prompts tab is built the same way.

3 A few habits that make prompts better

Be specific about the output.

Ask for a table, a number of bullets, a word count, or a tone. Vague in, vague out.

Give it your content.

Type / to point Copilot at a specific email, meeting, file, or person, so it works from your real information instead of guessing.

Iterate — it's a conversation.

The first answer is a starting point. Follow up with "make it shorter," "more formal," or "add a section on risks." Copilot remembers the thread.

Always review.

Copilot gives you a fast first draft, not the final word. Check names, numbers, and dates before you send or share.

Agents 101

Build your own Copilot agent — no code required

An agent is a custom version of Copilot you set up once and reuse forever. You give it a name, a job description, and instructions for how it should behave — and optionally point it at trusted files or sites for knowledge. Agent Builder lets you create one just by describing it in plain language.

Choose your role

1 Why build an agent?

Consistency

Your best prompt, captured once. Everyone on the team gets the same high-quality help.

Speed

No re-typing long instructions. Open the agent and go.

Grounded

Point it at the right SharePoint sites or files so answers use your team's source of truth.

Shareable

Build it once, then share it with your peers across the leadership office.

2 How to build one in Agent Builder

  1. Open Agent Builder. In Microsoft 365 Copilot, choose Create agent (New agent) to launch the builder.
  2. Describe it in plain language. Paste a description like the starter prompts below. Copilot fills in the name, description, and instructions for you.
  3. Refine conversationally. Tweak the tone, add a rule, change the name — just keep typing what you want.
  4. Add the knowledge — this is the key step. The agent only knows what you give it here. Point it at the SharePoint library (or OneDrive files) that holds your meeting notes, agendas, pre-reads, and the objective scorecard — you can also add specific Teams chats or emails. Add these a day or two ahead so they finish indexing.
  5. Add starter prompts. Give users one-click example prompts so they know what to ask.
  6. Test it. Try it in the preview pane on the right and adjust until it feels right.
  7. Create & share. Save the agent and share it with the colleagues who'd benefit.
What an agent can — and can't — reach. An agent answers only from the knowledge sources you add — SharePoint and OneDrive files and sites, specific Teams chats, specific emails, approved websites, and connectors. It does not automatically read your calendar or your whole mailbox, so it won't see past meeting notes unless those notes live in a knowledge source you've attached. For a brief that pulls live from your calendar and recent email instead, use the Copilot "Prepare for your next meeting" prompt on the Prompts tab (Executive Admin).

When to use what

Which Copilot should you reach for?

The same task often works in more than one place. This is a recommendation, not a rule — it points you at the most convenient option and explains why. As you move left to right, you trade speed for depth.

1 The five choices

1 · Copilot Chat

Conversational AI for drafting, Q&A, and ideation. You prompt, it responds, and you drive the conversation — answers come back in seconds. Best for quick drafts, summaries, brainstorming, translating, and looking things up.Where: Copilot chat

2 · In the app

Copilot inside the open document, in editing mode — so it works on your real file, your real thread, your real numbers. Best for drafting in Word, analyzing data in Excel, building slides in PowerPoint, and writing email in Outlook.Where: Word · Excel · PowerPoint · Outlook · Teams

3 · Build an agent

A personal or team agent for specialized work and repeatable processes. You capture the job once — instructions plus the documents it should answer from — and everyone runs it the same way every time.Where: Agent Builder

4 · Researcher & Analyst

3 What each one looks like in practice

The same five choices, as prompts you can copy. Notice how the shape of the prompt changes with the tool.

Use Copilot Chat when you need an answer, not an artifact

Microsoft 365 Copilot
Catch me up on [the initiative]. Pull the key updates, decisions, and open risks from my email and Teams over the last week, tell me what's waiting on me, and flag anything that looks like it's slipping. Keep it to eight bullets.
Microsoft 365 Copilot
Brainstorm ten ways we could frame [this change] for our clients. For each one, give me a one-line headline and who it would land best with. Don't write the full message yet — I want to pick a direction first.

Use Copilot in the app when the work is the file

Outlook
Draft a reply to this thread that confirms our position, asks for the two data points we're still missing, and proposes a call on Thursday. Keep it under 150 words and match the tone of the thread.
Excel
Analyze this workbook and tell me which lines moved most versus last quarter. Add a column showing the variance as a percentage, highlight anything that moved more than ten percent, and give me a two-sentence summary of what's driving the change.
PowerPoint
Turn [the attached document] into a six-slide deck: the situation, the three options, the recommendation, the risks, the cost, and next steps. Keep the wording tight enough to read from the back of the room.

Build an agent when you're pasting the same prompt over and over

The signal is repetition. If you're re-typing a long instruction more than about once a week, or your whole team needs the same answer in the same shape, that's an agent — not a prompt.

Agent Builder
Create an agent called Control Review Assistant that answers questions about our control standards and our review process. Ground it in its knowledge: our control standards, procedure documents, and the review checklist from our SharePoint library. When I describe a change or a new initiative, tell me which controls apply, what evidence the reviewers will expect, and what's most likely to get challenged. Answer only from the documents, separate fact from recommendation, and if the documents don't cover something, tell me plainly what's missing instead of guessing. Give it a starter prompt that reads: Which controls apply to:
Full walkthrough on the Agents 101 tab. It covers how to build one step by step in Agent Builder, what an agent can and can't reach, and a ready-made starter agent for your role.

Use Researcher or Analyst when the thinking is the hard part

Both are ready-to-use specialists with their own reasoning models. Call them by typing @ in Copilot chat — they'll take longer than a normal chat answer, because they're doing considerably more.

@Researcher
Research [the topic, firm, or market] and build me a briefing: what's happened over the last twelve months, the key drivers behind it, how it connects to our own priorities, and the two or three implications for my team. Use both our internal material and the web, separate fact from interpretation, and cite your sources.
@Analyst
Analyze [attach the workbook] and work out [the question]. Show me the trend over time, break it down by [the dimension that matters], call out anything that looks like an outlier, and give me a chart. Tell me what you did so I can check the working.

Use Cowork when the deliverable outgrows a single prompt

Cowork keeps a persistent, shared workspace for a deliverable so you and Copilot can build it up over several sessions, and colleagues can pick up where you left off.

Cowork
Let's build out the [quarterly business review pack / client onboarding tracker / offsite briefing document] in this workspace. Start with an outline based on what we discussed, and we'll add sections, data, and edits together over the next few sessions. Keep a running list of open questions we still need to resolve.
